# Gen Re — Tone & Voice Reference

For use by the LinkedIn content-generator skill and any other Cowork skill drafting external-facing
copy about Gen Re. Synthesized from Gen Re's own public materials and prior client-context research
(`CLIENT.md`) — not invented. Re-verify against Gen Re's actual Corporate Communications/Marketing
team before this drives anything that actually publishes; treat this file as a well-informed starting
point, not a substitute for their sign-off.

## Who's being addressed

Gen Re is a ~180-year-old, dual-continent reinsurer (General Reinsurance Corporation, US, 1921;
General Reinsurance AG, Germany, via Cologne Re, 1846) and a wholly owned Berkshire Hathaway company.
Content that reads as move-fast startup enthusiasm will land badly here. Content framed as durable
craft and better-supported judgment will land.

## Core voice principles

1. **Understate and evidence. Avoid hype.** Do not promise efficiency multipliers or use
   superlative/breathless language. The Berkshire posture is "lose money and I'll be understanding;
   lose reputation and I'll be ruthless" — reputational conservatism is a stated value, not a
   compliance checkbox.
2. **Price is what you pay. Value is what you get.** — Buffett framing already used on Gen Re's own
   site. A safe register to echo: durability and judgment over speed or novelty.
3. **"As your reinsurer, we do not merely assume your risks — we help you manage them."** Gen Re's
   own positioning statement. It calls itself an underwriting company, not a market-driven capacity
   provider.
4. **Underwriting discipline over volume.** Gen Re states it prioritizes underwriting quality over
   premium growth and will walk away from underpriced business. Any content touching competitive
   performance should frame discipline as the differentiator, not scale for its own sake.
5. **Long-term client view.** Relationships are measured in decades, not renewal cycles. Avoid
   quarter-to-quarter urgency language ("act now," "don't miss out") — it's the wrong register for
   this brand entirely.
6. **Tagline:** *"The People Behind the Promise®"* — usable in signage/sign-off contexts, not as a
   hashtag-style slogan bolted onto every post.

## Vocabulary rules — non-negotiable

- Say **"reinsurer," "cedent," "treaty," "facultative," "submission," "experience," "cession"** — and
  use them correctly. Reinsurance vocabulary used wrong costs credibility instantly with this
  audience.
- **Never** say "customers" — Gen Re's customers are insurance companies, called **cedents** or
  **clients**.
- **"Associates,"** not "employees" — Gen Re's own word for its people.
- **"Gen Re"** — two words, capital R. Not "GenRe," not "Gen-Re."
- Legal entity names when precision matters: **General Reinsurance Corporation** (US),
  **General Reinsurance AG** (Germany).

## What "positive" means for this brand

Positive framing here is **evidenced confidence, not promotion.** A LinkedIn post that says "Gen Re
crushed the competition this quarter" is off-brand and will read as overselling to an audience that
prizes verifiable claims. A post that says "disciplined underwriting continued to show up in the
numbers this quarter" is on-brand. Every performance claim in generated content should be traceable
to a specific figure in the source report — the same "source every claim" discipline this program
teaches for internal work applies doubly to anything with Gen Re's name on it in public.

## Draft-then-review, always

Every skill built against this voice guide should produce a **draft for Corporate
Communications/Marketing (and Legal, given the public-company-adjacent sensitivity of competitive
commentary) to review before anything publishes** — this is the same "Cowork drafts, you sign off"
principle the whole onsite program teaches, applied to external content where the stakes of getting
it wrong are highest.
