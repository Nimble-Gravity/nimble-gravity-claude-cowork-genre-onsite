Get Set Up

Five things to get right before anyone delegates anything: the app is installed, you're signed in on your Gen Re account, you can find Cowork, you're on the same build as the projector, and you're running Sonnet. Then a look at what personalizing Cowork actually produces — you'll run that yourself in Lesson 4.

Foundations & Personal Value · ~15 min
01 — Get the app running

Same screen, same starting line

Do these five in order. They take about five minutes on a machine that's already been through IT, and they're the reason the next three days don't stall on somebody's install.

1
Install the Claude desktop app — or confirm it's already on your machine.
Cowork lives only in the desktop app. The browser version of Claude is chat, and chat can't open your files.
Claude opens in its own window with its own taskbar icon, not in a browser tab.
2
Sign in with your Gen Re work account.
Your licence and your organization's settings ride on that account — a personal login gets you a different, unlicensed Claude.
Your Gen Re workspace name shows in the account area of the app.
3
Click Cowork in the left rail.
Chat and Cowork are two different modes of the same app. Everything for the next three days happens in Cowork, so knowing where it lives is not a detail.
Cowork appears in the left rail and opens a folder picker rather than a plain chat box. If it isn't there, flag a facilitator now — it's a licence setting, not something you can fix from your seat.
4
Check your app version against the one on the projector.
An older build moves menus around, which is how a room ends up debugging three different screens at once. If yours is behind, update before we go any further.
Your version matches or beats the projector's.
5
Set the model selector to Sonnet.
Sonnet handles almost everything Cowork is asked to do at a fraction of the cost of Opus, so it's the working default everywhere at Gen Re. Day 2 covers the few jobs worth stepping up for.
The model selector next to the task box reads Sonnet.
02 — What personalizing produces

A few plain files that travel with you

Out of the box Cowork is capable and generic. It gets specific once it knows your role, who you write for, and what "good" looks like in your team. You don't fill in a form to do that — an interview asks you questions and writes these files from your answers. You'll run it in Lesson 4; this is what it leaves behind.

claude.md

A short file Cowork reads at the start of every session in that folder. It holds your standing context — who you are, what you're accountable for, and where to find the rest — which is what gets drafts sounding like you from the first reply.

An about-me/ folder

Five files covering your role and tools (about-me), how you write (voice-profile), what to never do in your name (writing-rules), and your people and current work (team and memory, which Cowork keeps current as you go).

📄
They're just text files — open them

Nothing here is a hidden setting. Everything Cowork "knows" about you sits in files you can open, read, and edit in Notepad or Word. If a draft comes back wrong, the fix is usually one line in voice-profile.md or writing-rules.md — not a better prompt.

03 — How the interview works

It asks, you answer, it writes

Left to run, the interview takes 20 to 40 minutes. In Lesson 4 you'll run the first pass only — about five minutes of questions, then you tell it to write the files with what it has, and you leave with a working personalization. Finishing the rest is homework. Three things worth knowing before you start.

01
One question at a time
Questions arrive in a popup with answer choices where they help, so most of it is picking rather than typing. It waits for each answer before asking the next, and it sharpens vague answers with a follow-up.
02
It follows your role
An underwriter, an actuary, a claims manager, and a finance lead should get four different conversations. Answer the "what do you actually do" question carefully — everything after it follows from your answer.
03
You can skip anything
Say "skip" and it leaves a [FILL IN] marker you can find later. It never invents an answer on your behalf. A setup with three gaps beats one you abandon halfway.
✅
Before it writes anything, it reads back

The interview recaps what it heard and lets you correct it before a single file is written. Read that recap properly — it's faster to fix a misunderstanding there than to notice it three drafts later.

04 — Microsoft 365 connector

Conceptual today, useful later

Anthropic ships a Microsoft 365 connector that lets Claude reach your mail, calendar, and SharePoint or OneDrive files. It is not live at Gen Re today, and whether it arrives during the pilot is a Gen Re decision. Either way nothing in these three days depends on it — everything runs from the local workshop folder.

What it would add

Context you'd otherwise paste in

With the connector on, the personalization interview can read how you actually write from your sent mail and pre-fill half its questions, and a later task can pull the submission straight from SharePoint instead of you saving a copy to a folder first.

Conceptual until the connector is live at Gen Re.
What it doesn't change

It sees only what you can see

The connector uses delegated permissions: Claude inherits your access, never more, and those calls land in the Microsoft 365 audit log like any other access to those files. It needs an admin to turn it on for the organization — it isn't something you enable from your own settings.

Nothing today depends on it.
📁
Local folder first, always

Even once the connector lands, the habit worth keeping is the one you'll learn in the next lesson: put the files a task needs into one folder and point Cowork at that folder. A narrow grant is easier to explain, easier to review, and easier to undo.
