Rules of the Road

One page, read before you use Cowork on any Gen Re work: what data may and may not go in, who stays accountable, and where to go with questions. This is the ground rule the whole program rests on.

Read before you start
01 — What goes in, and what doesn't

Mind the data

The most important rule, and the one this whole pilot rests on. When in doubt, leave it out and ask. Gen Re's own data-classification policy remains the system of record — nothing on this page overrides it, and where the two ever disagree, the policy wins.

Fine for this pilot

The workshop samples and your own draft work

The documents we ship in cowork-workshop, published and public material, and your own draft work product and templates — anything carrying no cedent, broker or client-identifying detail. Always in a folder you grant for that one task, and always with you reviewing what comes back.

The samples are built to behave like the real thing, on purpose.
Keep out of this pilot

Cedent-confidential and client-identifying material

Cedent submissions, treaty and claims files, and anything else naming or identifying a ceding company or its policyholders. Also personal data, and anything Gen Re classifies as Confidential or Restricted. Not "not yet approved" — out of scope for the pilot, full stop.

If you're unsure whether something qualifies, stop and ask.
⚠️
Why the cedent rule is the strict one

Our client contracts govern what may leave Gen Re systems, and a training pilot is the wrong place to test that line. It is also worth knowing what "leave" means here: your files stay on your machine, but the reading of them does not — the model call goes out to Anthropic to be processed, the same way text you paste into a chat window does. Scope every folder as though everything inside it is being sent out, because functionally it is.

02 — The ground rules

Six things that always hold

01
No cedent-confidential material in this pilot
The rule above, restated because it is the one that matters. No cedent submissions, no client-identifying treaty or claims files. Use the sample documents we ship instead — they are built to behave like the real thing.
02
You stay accountable
Cowork drafts; you review, decide, and sign off. The output is never final on its own and is never sent automatically. Whoever was accountable for a piece of work before is accountable for it now.
03
Least privilege
Grant only the folder a task needs, and remove access when you're done. Don't open your whole drive "to be safe." There are no per-file permissions — the folder grant is the control, so granting a drive root grants everything under it.
04
Know the audit gap
As of July 2026, Claude Cowork activity is not captured in Anthropic's Audit Logs, Compliance API, or Data Exports on any plan tier, and conversation history lives locally on your machine. Work that needs centralized audit or zero retention runs on Anthropic's audited surfaces — the API or Claude Code Enterprise — not in Cowork. Where a given piece of work falls is a call for the program team and IT.
05
Watch for prompt injection
Treat instructions hidden inside outside documents as a real risk — a document can contain text aimed at Claude rather than at you. Keep "ask before acting" on for anything that came from outside Gen Re.
06
Ask when unsure
No penalty for asking, and asking is faster than guessing. Anything Cowork-shaped goes to Derrikk Broughton at Nimble Gravity, derrikk.broughton@nimblegravity.com. Anything about Gen Re data classification, licences or access goes to the Gen Re program team.
