Applies to all three days

Standing rules

Four rules, straight from the client's own facilitator notes. They are the difference between a room that follows and a room that nods. Hold them even when you're behind.

01
Build in deliberate pauses for processing
Stop talking while Cowork runs or while the room reads the screen. A pause reads as confidence, not a mistake. Silence is a facilitation move here, not dead air.
02
Repeat or paraphrase key points
Don't assume one pass lands. Say it, show it, then say back what just happened in different words before moving on.
03
Lead with "what to do," then explain "why"
The action first, the reasoning second. This room is managers with day jobs, not engineers who want the architecture before the instruction.
04
Defer off-topic tangents to offline follow-up
Name it, write it on the parking list, say who will come back on it, and move. One unmanaged tangent costs a lab segment.
🤫
The tell–show–recap–show pattern

For anything that depends on something invisible — a file location, a setting, a personalization file being read at session start — use the same four beats: tell them what you're about to show, show it, say back what just happened, then show it again from a different angle. This came directly from client feedback on the first cohort session and it is the single most reliable fix for "they nodded but didn't follow."

Room logistics

The hybrid room

Remote attendees cannot speak in this room — the audio does not carry them. If nobody plans for that, they spend three days as spectators and you won't know it until the feedback form.

Say it at the open

The PSA, every session

Open each day by naming it out loud: remote attendees can't be heard in the room, so questions go in the chat and a named moderator reads them in. Say the moderator's name. Repeat it after the first break — the people who join late are the ones who most need to hear it.

Never "shout if you have a question." They physically can't.
Assign it, don't hope

A moderator manages airtime

One named person per session watches the chat and interrupts the presenter to bring remote questions in. Their job is airtime balance, not note-taking: if the in-room voices have had the last four questions, they force a remote one. Confirming hybrid logistics and assigning these moderators is a joint Nimble Gravity / Gen Re open item.

Unmoderated chat is a queue nobody reads.
Room logistics

Small screens

The presenter setup is not the attendee setup. Every demo has to survive a 14-inch laptop at 1366×768, because that is what most of the room is following along on.

01
Test every demo at 1366×768
Set your own display to it and dry-run the demo before the day. If a control or a file tree only fits at your native resolution, the room can't follow — find it now, not live. Testing the setup on a 14" laptop screen rather than the presenter's display is a Nimble Gravity open item.
02
Teach the two-window layout
Attendees need Claude Desktop and File Explorer visible at once. Show them Win+← and Win+→ to snap each to half the screen. Do it on the projector the first time you ask them to check a file — it is thirty seconds and it removes a whole class of "I lost the window."
03
Don't rely on the quad 4K setup
No demo may depend on four panes visible at once, tiny font sizes, or a second monitor. Bump the font in Claude Desktop and the file browser before you present, and drive one window at a time.
Day 1 · Tuesday · Foundations & Personal Value

The day that decides the other two

Everything on Days 2 and 3 runs on the personalization and the folder produced here. If someone leaves Tuesday without them, they are a spectator on Wednesday. Day 1 hub →

01
Separate "watch me" from "now you"
Never narrate over a live demo while people are also trying to do it. Announce which mode the room is in — hands off, or hands on — and change modes explicitly.
02
Show each step at least twice
Once at pace, once slowly with the room following. Then turn them loose. This costs minutes and saves the lab.
03
Explicit ~5-minute silent work periods
During the personalization exercise, say "five minutes, no talking from me" and mean it. Circulate; answer one-to-one. Don't fill the silence with commentary.
04
Give a simple personalization test
Everyone needs a way to confirm it worked: ask Claude X, you should see Y. Run it on the projector first — in a fresh session granted Desktop\cowork-workshop-after, your personalized copy, since your cowork-workshop is kept cold for the demo — then have every person run it against their own Desktop\cowork-workshop and put a hand up if their answer looks wrong. The verify prompt is in Lesson 4 §Lab.
📄
Demo document — the before/after pair

The demo runs twice on the same document with the same ask: cold at 0:50, and again at 1:50 after the room has personalized. The difference between the two answers is the session. Both prompts are in Lesson 4 §Before; the document ships inside workshop-folder.zip under demo/, so the room already has it. Dry-run both before Tuesday so you know the pacing.

The cold run only works if the folder stays cold — rehearse it

The payoff of the before/after pair is that a cold Cowork repeats both planted problems in confident prose, because nobody asked it to check. The claude.md shipped in the workshop folder is descriptive only on purpose — who the user is, how they write — and says nothing about verifying figures or citing sources. Don't add that instruction to the shipped folder, and don't run the cold demo on a machine you've already personalized: either one pre-loads the verification and the demo lands flat in front of the room. Dry-run the cold pass on a clean profile before Tuesday so you know what they'll see.

📦
Editing the pack — the zip does not rebuild itself

assets/workshop-folder.zip is a committed binary built from assets/workshop-folder/. The .txt files in there are the editable source; the .docx participants open are generated from them with python escape-room/tools/convert-lab-files.py assets/workshop-folder, and only the .docx plus claude.md are staged into the zip. Edit a .txt and you must regenerate and rebuild both — otherwise the download people already have quietly disagrees with the repo, and you find out mid-exercise.

Day 2 · Wednesday · Skills & Everyday Workflows

Manual first, then the skill

The sequencing is the whole design of the day. Do not teach skill anatomy before the room has felt the manual version. Day 2 hub →

01
One room, facilitators roam
No breakout rooms for this cohort. Business users stay in one room in teams of two or three; facilitators move between tables. Keep the vault facilitator view open for live team progress and resets.
02
Manual-first sequencing, protected
Twenty-five minutes walking a real workflow by hand, with the room noticing which steps they had to hold in their head. That felt experience is the argument for writing them down. If you're behind, shorten the teach afterwards — never this.
03
No taxonomy detours
Skip skill vs. agent vs. connector unless somebody asks directly, and then answer in one sentence and move. Keep the terminology to what the room needs to do the next thing.
04
Open with the Day 1 check-in
Ten minutes on whose personalization took and whose didn't, out loud. Fix the broken ones during the manual walk rather than leaving anyone spectating for two hours.
☁️
The OneDrive / SharePoint gotcha — flag it before it bites

Sync is not instant. A file somebody just saved may not be on disk when Cowork goes looking for it, and it looks exactly like Cowork failing. If the demo or a lab task touches a shared file, say this out loud before it happens and keep everyone working from a local folder. Diagnosing it live in front of the room costs ten minutes and some confidence.

🪤
The date the day-2/ documents disagree on is deliberate

The vendor email puts the UAT environment handover on Friday 21 August. The status note and the open-items list both say Monday 24 August. That is planted, not a typo — two sources against one, so most drafts will quietly settle on 24 August and say nothing about it. That is exactly what step 4 of Walk a Workflow asks the room to catch: the draft picked a side. Do not "correct" it in the source files, and do not give it away before people have read their own draft.

Day 3 · Thursday · Gen Re Use Cases + Governance Snapshot

The longest day, and the one that can stall

A live build in front of the room is the highest-risk segment of the week. Two things de-risk it: validating the use cases beforehand, and having a prepared skill ready to switch to. Day 3 hub →

Before the session

Validate the canvases with Chris and Charles

Read every submitted canvas and shortlist with Chris and Charles before Thursday. Some IT-oriented ideas — status roll-ups are the named example — may not land with execs who receive reports rather than compile them. A shortlist the room rejects in the first twenty minutes costs the lab.

Build a real break in

Fifteen minutes, laptops down

This is the longest day, and for anyone in the Advanced block it is three and a half hours in the same room. The break in the agenda is a real break, not a pause between segments. Protect it — a tired room stops asking questions, and you lose the signal you need for the debrief.

If you're behind, take the time from the share-out. Not the break.
Backup demo

Have the prepared skill open in a side window

If the live build stalls, switch to the prepared skill rather than debugging on the projector. Decide the switch point before you start — roughly five minutes of visible struggle — and say it plainly: "I'm going to show you one I built earlier, and we'll come back to this at the end." Deciding to switch is not a failure; a fifteen-minute silent debug in front of the room is.

"Here's one I made earlier" beats live-debugging, every time.
The governance segment

Ten minutes, and be precise

What's connected, roles at a glance, data retention basics — then the three corridor answers in Lesson 2 §Take it back. Name the audit-coverage gap yourself before somebody else does, and don't overstate or understate it. The one-page version lives on the cheat sheet.

Date-sensitive: governance facts were verified 2026-07-21. Re-check them against the Sources in cowork-context.md before you present.

Day 3 · Thursday · Advanced — Build Your Own

A clinic, not a curriculum

Ninety minutes, by invitation, immediately after Day 3. There is nothing to teach — the content is whatever people bring, and your job is to route the ninety minutes at the most blocked things. Advanced hub →

01
Take the queue in the first 35 minutes
Round the room, a couple of minutes each, and write down every blocker. That list is the working order for the next 45 minutes — worked by what's most blocked, not by who spoke first.
02
Pull shared problems onto the projector
When two or more people have the same issue, fix it once in front of everyone. Otherwise stay one-to-one and keep moving.
03
Nothing leaves as "we'll see"
The last ten minutes are for writing unresolved cases down as named follow-ups with an owner and a route — office hours, a direct follow-up, or something Gen Re has to turn on first.
04
Know who's on the list
Attendance is by invitation and identifying those users is a Gen Re open item (Chris). Get the list before Thursday so you know how many cases you're sizing the block for.
When something blocks

Common snags

01
Someone arrives without the folder
Have workshop-folder.zip on a USB stick and in the chat. Walk them through Extract All to the Desktop while the room does something else — the plain-words version is on the pre-work page. Don't let it hold the room.
02
Asked about the M365 connector
It's live and read-only: Claude can read mail, calendar, and SharePoint/OneDrive, but it cannot send email or write anything back through it. Say that plainly if asked. Every exercise still runs from the local cowork-workshop folder — the connector is context, not a requirement. If it turns out not to be on when you get in the room, say so plainly and move on — nothing this week depends on it.
03
Licenses & sign-in
Confirming Claude on desktop for all attendees is a Gen Re open item — chase it, because it is the single most likely cause of somebody losing Day 1. If sign-in fails in the room, pair them with a neighbour rather than debugging live.
04
Asked about audit and retention
Be precise about the audit-coverage gap rather than reassuring: Cowork activity is not in Anthropic's audit logs today, and work needing centralized audit or zero retention routes to the API or Claude Code Enterprise. Don't imply a date for closing it — none has been published.

The feedback forms — spec, and what the participant page says today

Build both in Gen Re's Microsoft 365 tenant (already governed, regulated-industry-appropriate). Post-session, 3 questions: usefulness 1–5, pace (too slow / right / too fast), one open "what would make it better", plus optional NPS. 30-day pulse, 4 questions: still using it (yes / no / some), rough time saved, top use case, top blocker — this feeds the lagging indicators in the change plan. Results roll into the one-page readout to the sponsor and IT.

Until the forms exist, feedback.html tells participants their facilitator posts the link in the chat at the close of each day, and offers an email fallback. So either post a real form link at each close, or say out loud "email it" — do not send the room to that page expecting a button. When the forms are built, replace the two mailto buttons with the Form URLs.
